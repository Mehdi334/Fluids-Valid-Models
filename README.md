<h1> Axisymmetirc Diffuser Investigation</h1>


<h2>Description</h2>
I used emperically derived equations, experimatation and computational fluid dynamics simulation to investigate the effect of changing the cone angle and Reynolds number has on the pressure recovery coefficiency of an axisymmetric diffuser. The chalange with this project is to understand the limitations of each method and then to know how to incorporate the data from different sources to give a final verdict. The emperically derived equation relied on assumptions that are not enitrely accurate for our case, the experimental method is susceptible to uncertainty from the measuring equipment, and the CFD simulations utilise turbulence models and do not solve directly. The applicatinos of this project include diffusers in Formula 1 cars, and diffusers in gas turbines. Understanding how the cone angle and Reynolds number can affect the PRC is importatnt in allowing for the design of a more efficient diffuser. A high PRC means lower losses and it is achieved by having the optimal combination of Cone angle and Reynolds number to minimise skin friction surface area whilst ensuring separation does not occur.
